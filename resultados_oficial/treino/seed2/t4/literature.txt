[2411.11020v2] Label noise is a critical challenge in graph node classification tasks, and this paper introduces the Label Ensemble Graph Neural Network (LEGNN) to address it. LEGNN reframes label noise as a label ensemble task, gathering multiple informative labels instead of relying on a single reliable label. This approach reduces computational complexity compared to existing methods that use quadratic complexity for label reliability assessment. The paper evaluates LEGNN on graph datasets with label noise and demonstrates its effectiveness in improving classification accuracy under noisy label conditions. The results highlight the importance of ensemble-based strategies for robust learning in the presence of label noise.
[2008.00235v1] This paper addresses the challenges of building classification models for high-dimensional multi-omics data, where traditional logistic regression with elastic-net penalty often leads to poor results. The authors propose a two-step penalised logistic regression approach, where variable selection is performed separately on each omic layer, followed by the construction of a predictive model using the selected variables. The method is evaluated on cardiometabolic syndrome datasets and is compared to other existing approaches, demonstrating its effectiveness in handling multi-omic data with high noise and varying predictor characteristics. The results highlight the importance of tailored variable selection strategies for multi-omic classification tasks.
[2112.02962v4] DANets introduces Deep Abstract Networks for tabular data classification and regression, proposing a novel neural component called Abstract Layer (AbstLay) that explicitly groups correlative input features to generate higher-level semantic features. The paper also introduces a structure re-parameterization method to reduce computational complexity. The model is evaluated on various tabular datasets and demonstrates strong performance, particularly in handling complex relationships between features. This paper is relevant to the topic as it explores methods for tabular classification, which is a key aspect of the research question.
[2012.11854v2] This paper addresses the challenges posed by instance-dependent label noise in deep learning, where the probability of a label error is not uniform across all data instances but depends on the task difficulty. The authors propose a second-order approach to learning under such noise conditions, which accounts for the variability in label noise across different instances. The method is evaluated on various datasets, and the results demonstrate its effectiveness in improving model performance in the presence of instance-dependent label noise. This paper is relevant to the topic as it explores the impact of label noise on model training, which is central to the research question.
