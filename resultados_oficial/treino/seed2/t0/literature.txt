[2503.06054v1] Fine-Grained Bias Detection in LLM: Enhancing detection mechanisms for nuanced biases
This paper introduces a detection framework for identifying nuanced biases in Large Language Models (LLMs). The framework integrates contextual analysis, interpretability via attention mechanisms, and counterfactual data augmentation to capture hidden biases across linguistic contexts. The methodology employs contrastive prompts and synthetic datasets to analyze model behavior across cultural, ideological, and demographic scenarios. Quantitative and qualitative analyses demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed approach in detecting subtle biases that may influence decision-making and reinforce stereotypes. The study highlights the importance of fine-grained bias detection in ensuring the ethical and fair use of LLMs.
[2406.02050v4] JBBQ: Japanese Bias Benchmark for Analyzing Social Biases in Large Language Models
This paper introduces the Japanese Bias Benchmark for Question Answering (JBBQ), an extension of the English bias benchmark BBQ, designed to analyze social biases in Japanese large language models (LLMs). The study reveals that while Japanese LLMs with more parameters show improved accuracy on JBBQ, their bias scores increase. The paper also explores mitigation strategies such as prompts with warnings about social biases and chain-of-thought prompting, which reduce the effect of biases in model outputs. However, the study highlights the need for further improvements in extracting correct evidence from model outputs to address biases effectively. The findings emphasize the importance of analyzing social biases in multilingual LLMs and provide a benchmark for future research in this area.
[2601.03730v1] Perception-Aware Bias Detection for Query Suggestions
This paper addresses the issue of bias in query suggestions within web search, which has received limited attention in bias detection research. The study builds on the bias detection pipeline developed by Bonart et al. for person-related search, but introduces perception-aware metrics to overcome challenges such as the sparseness of query suggestions and the lack of contextual metadata. Since query suggestions are perceived briefly and subliminally, the paper emphasizes the importance of metrics that align with human perception. The enhanced pipeline is demonstrated to better detect biases in query suggestions, contributing to the broader field of bias detection in AI systems. The findings highlight the need for context-aware and perception-sensitive approaches in evaluating and mitigating biases in search-related outputs.
[2410.16927v1] Revealing Hidden Bias in AI: Lessons from Large Language Models
This paper investigates the presence of biases in candidate interview reports generated by several large language models (LLMs), including Claude 3.5 Sonnet, GPT-4o, Gemini 1.5, and Llama 3.1 405B. The study focuses on biases related to gender, race, and age, highlighting how these biases can influence decision-making processes. The paper evaluates the effectiveness of LLM-based anonymization techniques in mitigating such biases, finding that while anonymization reduces certain biases—particularly gender bias—the degree of reduction varies across models and bias types. Notably, Llama 3.1 405B exhibited the lowest overall bias. The study provides insights into the hidden biases within LLMs and proposes a methodology for assessing inherent biases beyond recruitment applications, contributing to the broader understanding of bias in AI systems.
