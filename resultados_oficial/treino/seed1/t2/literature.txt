[2406.13677v3] Leveraging Large Language Models to Measure Gender Representation Bias in Gendered Language Corpora: This paper addresses the issue of gender representation bias in training corpora for large language models (LLMs). It highlights that while prior work has focused on gender stereotyping bias, gender representation bias - the unequal frequency of references to individuals of different genders - has received less attention. The paper proposes a novel LLM-based method to detect and quantify gender representation bias in language corpora, emphasizing its importance as an upstream source of bias that can propagate and intensify throughout the model lifecycle.
[2507.16557v1] Exploring Gender Bias in Large Language Models: An In-depth Dive into the German Language: This paper investigates gender bias in large language models (LLMs) with a focus on the German language. It highlights the challenges in transferring bias measurement methods developed for English to other languages, and presents five German datasets for gender bias evaluation in LLMs. The datasets are based on well-established gender bias concepts and are accessible through multiple methodologies. The paper analyzes eight multilingual LLM models and identifies unique challenges in German, including the ambiguous interpretation of male occupational terms and the influence of neutral nouns on gender perception. This work contributes to the understanding of gender bias in multilingual settings and its implications for fairness in NLP applications.
[2207.10245v1] The Birth of Bias: A case study on the evolution of gender bias in an English language model: This paper investigates how language models become biased during their training process. Using a relatively small language model based on the LSTM architecture trained on an English Wikipedia corpus, the authors analyze how gender bias develops over time. They find that gender representation is dynamic and identify different phases during training that contribute to the emergence of bias. The study provides insights into how bias is encoded in the model's internal state and how it relates to downstream tasks such as semantic textual similarity. This work contributes to understanding the origins of gender bias in language models and highlights the importance of monitoring bias development throughout the training process.
