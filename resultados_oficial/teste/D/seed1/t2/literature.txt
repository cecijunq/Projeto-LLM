[1712.01794v1] This paper investigates the effect of negators, modals, and degree adverbs on sentiment composition. The authors created a dataset of phrases with various modifiers and annotated them with real-valued sentiment scores. Their analysis shows that the impact of modifiers varies significantly, and individual modifiers within the same group can have different effects on sentiment. The study provides insights into how these linguistic elements influence sentiment, which is crucial for understanding how sentiment classifiers handle negation in text.
[2107.11597v1] This paper focuses on negation handling in machine learning-based sentiment classification for colloquial Arabic, highlighting the challenges posed by negation in low-resource and morphologically rich languages. The authors propose a rule-based approach to address negation, leveraging linguistic knowledge and sentiment lexicons. They evaluate their method on a dataset of colloquial Arabic reviews and demonstrate that incorporating negation handling significantly improves sentiment classification accuracy. This paper is relevant to our topic as it explores the impact of negation in short text sentiment analysis, particularly in multilingual and low-resource settings, which aligns with the broader goal of understanding how sentiment classifiers handle negation in short movie reviews.
[1906.07610v2] This paper proposes a multi-task learning approach to explicitly incorporate information about negation in sentiment analysis, which outperforms implicit negation learning in a data-driven manner. The authors demonstrate that identifying negation and disentangling its effect on the final polarity of a text is crucial for accurate sentiment prediction. The approach is evaluated on short texts, including movie reviews, and shows significant improvements in handling negation, making it highly relevant to our research topic on the sensitivity of sentiment classifiers to negation words in short movie reviews (SST-2).
